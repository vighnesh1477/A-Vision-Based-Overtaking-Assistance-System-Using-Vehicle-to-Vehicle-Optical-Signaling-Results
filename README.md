# Practical Video Evaluation Results

This repository provides the processed video results used for the practical evaluation of the **Vision-Based Overtaking Assistance System Using Vehicle-to-Vehicle Optical Signaling**.

The videos are provided as supplementary material to demonstrate the behavior of the complete framework when processing continuous forward-facing driving video sequences. The evaluation includes **25 real driving video sequences** recorded using a forward-facing dash camera and **one extended driving sequence** recorded from *Euro Truck Simulator 2*.

The videos were processed using the same integrated framework used for the experimental evaluation reported in the accompanying research paper.

---

A total of **25 real driving video sequences** were evaluated using forward-facing dash-camera footage. Each sequence represents a continuous driving segment of approximately **2--4 minutes** and contains naturally occurring variations in road appearance, vehicle presence, road geometry, and environmental conditions.

## 2. Extended Simulator-Based Evaluation

In addition to the real driving footage, an extended continuous driving sequence was recorded using **Euro Truck Simulator 2 (ETS2)**.

The approximately **25-minute driving sequence** was recorded from the forward-facing driving view during a long-distance route and subsequently processed using the same framework and configuration used for the other evaluation videos.

This extended sequence was included to examine the temporal behavior and computational performance of the framework over a substantially longer continuous driving period.

> **Note:** The ETS2 sequence is considered a supplementary simulator-based evaluation and is not treated as a substitute for physical road testing.

## 3. Processing Information

All evaluation videos were processed through the complete overtaking-assistance pipeline, including the relevant perception, road analysis, environmental assessment, and decision-making components.

The observed processing frame rate across the evaluated video sequences was approximately **16--21 FPS**, depending on scene content and computational workload.

The purpose of these videos is to provide supplementary evidence of the framework's behavior on continuous video input and to support transparency and reproducibility of the practical evaluation described in the research paper.
